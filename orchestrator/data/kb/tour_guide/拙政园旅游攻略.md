# 拙政园旅游攻略

景点简介

https://m.suzhou.bendibao.com/jingdian/zhuozhengyuan/

拙政园是苏州最大、最著名的园林，全园以水为中心，萦绕着错落有致的假山及精致的庭院建筑，花木并茂。这一江南园林典范与北京 HYPERLINK "javascript:void(0)" 颐和园、 HYPERLINK "javascript:void(0)" 承德避暑山庄、苏州 HYPERLINK "javascript:void(0)" 留园并称为“中国四大名园”，并被列入世界文化遗产名录。

整个园林的设计十分精巧，游览起来可以说是“一步一景”，处处体现着江南水乡的韵味。园中小径曲折，从一重重门廊、镂空图案的石墙，到每个亭子、每扇窗户都不雷同，而且与树木花草搭配得恰到好处，构成一幅幅如画般的风景，你可以花上很多时间来摄影。建议租借一个导览器，了解这些美景背后的历史故事。

全园分为东、中、西三部分，各具特色，尤其中花园基本保持着明代园林朴素自然的风格，是全园最精华的，可以细细游玩。而东花园更加开阔，西花园建筑精美，在拙政园南端还建有 HYPERLINK "javascript:void(0)" 苏州园林博物馆（包含在拙政园门票内），是国内唯一的园林专题博物馆。

园内的主要景观有兰雪堂、涵青亭、远香堂、香洲、卅六鸳鸯馆、盆景园等。还有两个很有意思的名景，一是“北寺塔借景”，站在东花园往西看，可以见到1公里外的北寺塔，仿佛就在园子里，这是很多游客一定会拍照的景色，而借景的手法在园内也四处可见，你游玩时可以慢慢品味；二是中花园里的“廊桥小飞虹”，朱红色的桥栏倒映在水中宛如飞虹，以这座桥为主体的景观十分漂亮。

春、夏、秋三季都很适合游玩拙政园，3月中旬-5月上旬有杜鹃花展，6月上旬-10月中旬有荷花展，9月上旬-10月中旬有菊花展，春节期间还有花果盆景展。冬季去人会少很多，但可以安静地欣赏清秀的景色。

游玩季节

【拙政园最佳旅游季节】

春、夏、秋三季皆适宜游玩，四月至十月会举行花展(杜鹃花展和荷花展)。冬季景色略显逊色，但是人少，可以静静欣赏园林的美景。

【拙政园特色活动】

春：拙政园的“杜鹃花节”

每年春季，拙政园都要隆重举办一年一度的“杜鹃花节”。“杜鹃花节”既是拙政园的一项传统特色项目，也是被苏州市政府列入“苏州旅游节”的重点项目之一。杜鹃花展期间，将有数百种名贵杜鹃花展出。届时，拙政园内是莺歌燕舞，万紫千红，满目妖娆。不仅让游客在浓浓春意中领略游园的意趣，也让游客在山花烂漫中感受到拙政园这座城市山林的诗情画意。一、活动名称：苏州拙政园杜鹃花旅游节

二、举办时间：每年3月28日——5月10日

三、举办地点：拙政园内

夏：拙政园的“荷花节”

每年夏季，拙政园都将举办苏州园林的传统特色项目“荷花节”。“荷花节”依托于拙政园宽阔的荷塘，以数百个品种的缸荷、碗莲及多种水生植物造景，渲染荷花香远益清的感官效果，营造夏日江南水乡的清凉意境。此时的拙政园内，到处是‘青荷盖绿水，芙蓉披红鲜’的景象。在河堤绿荫下，在习习凉风中，拙政园成为游客赏荷的最佳去处，在拙政园观赏荷花展已成为苏州市民喜闻乐见的活动，成为了一种“新民俗”。

一、活动名称：苏州拙政园荷花旅游节

二、举办时间：每年6月上旬——10月中旬

三、举办地点：拙政园内

秋：拙政园的反季菊花展

每年秋季，拙政园都将以菊花为主题举办反季菊花展。菊花展中，如彩球，似花灯，像礼花，团团簇簇、千姿百态的菊花，展示着超凡脱俗，孤标亮节，高雅傲霜的自然特质。游客在以疏朗自然的风格著称的拙政园中赏菊，别有一番“采菊东篱下，悠悠见南山”的闲情逸趣。

一、活动名称：苏州拙政园反季菊花展

二、举办时间：每年9月上旬——10月中旬

三、举办地点：拙政园内

天气气候

　　苏州位于北亚热带湿润季风气候区，温暖潮湿多雨，季风明显，四季分明，冬夏季长，春秋季短。1月平均气温2-3℃，7月平均气温28℃，所以，如果您是初春或深秋来此旅游，最好在准备一件冬衣，以防突袭的寒气。

Humble Administrator's Garden Introduction

Basic Overview

The Humble Administrator's Garden is the largest and most renowned classical garden in Suzhou. Centered around waterscapes, it is dotted with layered rockeries, exquisite courtyard buildings and lush plants. As a masterpiece of Jiangnan gardens, it ranks among the Four Great Classical Gardens of China, together with the Summer Palace in Beijing, the Mountain Resort in Chengde and the Lingering Garden in Suzhou. It has also been inscribed on the World Heritage List.

Exquisitely designed, the garden features distinctive scenery at every step, fully embodying the charm of water towns in Jiangnan. Winding paths, layered corridors, hollowed stone walls, pavilions and windows all boast unique styles. Blended harmoniously with flowers and trees, they form picturesque views ideal for photography. It is recommended to rent an audio guide to learn about the history and stories behind the landscapes.

The garden is divided into three sections: the East Garden, the Central Garden and the West Garden, each with its own characteristics. The Central Garden retains the simple and natural style of gardens of the Ming Dynasty and is the essence of the whole scenic area, well worth a leisurely visit. The East Garden features a more open layout, while the West Garden is famous for its delicate architectures. Located at the southern end of the garden is the Suzhou Garden Museum, the only professional museum themed on classical gardens in China, and admission is included in the garden ticket.

Major scenic spots include the Lanxue Hall, Hanqing Pavilion, Xiangxiang Hall, Xiangzhou Boat, Thirty-Six Mandarin Ducks Hall and Bonsai Garden. Two iconic highlights are particularly impressive. One is the Borrowed View of North Temple Pagoda. Standing in the East Garden and looking west, visitors can catch sight of the North Temple Pagoda one kilometre away, which seems to stand right inside the garden. This borrowing technique can be seen everywhere across the garden. The other highlight is Flying Rainbow Bridge in the Central Garden. Its vermilion railings reflect on the water like a rainbow, creating a stunning landscape.

Spring, summer and autumn are the best seasons for a visit. The Azalea Show runs from mid-March to early May, the Lotus Show from early June to mid-October, and the Chrysanthemum Show from early September to mid-October. A flower and bonsai exhibition is also held during the Spring Festival. Though less bustling in winter, the garden presents serene and elegant views.

Reminders

The maximum capacity of the garden is around 9,000 people. It is advised to avoid weekends and public holidays to skip crowds.

Free guided tours are available at the tourist centre every half an hour. Self-service GPS audio guides can be rented at the small wooden pavilion near the west entrance for 20 yuan each.

Stamps, postcards and other souvenirs featuring garden scenery and Suzhou culture are sold at Shuxiang Hall. You can also have your postcards stamped and posted on site.

The Suzhou Museum stands right to the west of the garden. The Lion Grove Garden and Suzhou Folk Museum are also nearby for a combined tour.

There are parking lots in the south of the garden and a newly built one north of the east gate (opened in August 2014). Please park your vehicle in official parking areas. Do not park on roadside guided by illegal scalpers or unlicensed tour guides, nor buy tickets from them.

Recommended Visiting Seasons & Themed Events

Spring, summer and autumn are ideal for visiting. Flower exhibitions are held from April to October. The garden is quieter in winter for enjoying peaceful scenery.

Themed Activities

Azalea Festival (Late March – May 10) A traditional key event of Suzhou Tourism Festival. Hundreds of rare azalea varieties are on display, creating a vibrant spring scene full of poetic charm.

Lotus Festival (Early June – Mid-October) Dozens of lotus varieties and aquatic plants decorate the large ponds. The fragrant lotus blossoms form a refreshing summer landscape, which has become a popular local leisure activity.

Chrysanthemum Exhibition (Early September – Mid-October) Various chrysanthemums in full bloom showcase elegant and unyielding charm. Visitors can experience the tranquil leisure described in ancient poems while appreciating flowers here.

Climate & Travel Tips

Suzhou features a humid subtropical monsoon climate, with distinct four seasons, warm temperatures, abundant rainfall. January average temperature: 2–3℃; July average temperature: 28℃. It is suggested to bring warm clothes in early spring and late autumn against sudden cold snaps.

General Tips

The period from April to October is the best time to visit, when you can enjoy natural scenery and seasonal local specialties such as fresh peaches, Biluochun tea and candied lotus root.

Avoid weekends, public holidays and the Qingming Festival tomb-sweeping period to experience the authentic charm of classical gardens.

Handheld electronic audio guides are available for rent at the entrance.

Many craft shops selling local specialties, Suzhou fans and Suzhou embroidery line Dongbei Street and Yuanlin Road near the garden gate.

A 100-metre walk west from the garden leads to the Prince Zhong's Mansion, Suzhou Museum and Suzhou Garden Museum. The Lion Grove Garden and Suzhou Folk Museum are also within easy walking distance.

Photography Tips

Flower exhibitions are held from April to October. With dense green plants in the garden, a DSLR camera with high resolution is recommended for better shooting effects. Pay attention to light and shadow, and use flash for backlit scenes. Visit on less crowded days to capture panoramic and serene views.

Tips

The garden covers 78 mu (about 52,000 square meters), divided into four parts: eastern, central, western and residential areas. The residential area is a typical Suzhou dwelling, now used as the Suzhou Garden Museum. Most existing buildings were rebuilt in 1850 (the 9th year of Emperor Xianfeng of the Qing Dynasty) when the garden became part of the Prince Zhong’s Mansion. By the late Qing Dynasty, it had formed three relatively independent gardens: east, central and west.

Photography Tips

The garden has abundant green plants with strong color contrast with flowers. A high-resolution DSLR camera is recommended. Pay attention to light and shadow, and use flash fill light when shooting against the light.

The garden is very crowded during peak seasons, making it hard to capture full scenes. Visit during quiet periods to take peaceful, high-quality photos.
